The palace

A house of stone, light and long memory.

Behind a composed Pombaline façade at Rua do Ataíde 9, Palacio Bela Vista unfolds around a quadrangular plan on a long, narrow Lisbon plot.

The principal façade rises through three storeys. To the rear, where the house turns toward the garden and the river, there is a fourth. The rooms keep the quiet grammar of the eighteenth century: high stuccoed ceilings, carved doors, hand-painted azulejos and floors of black-and-white marble laid in a lozenge.

Light arrives from two sides for most of the day. Nothing has been over-restored; the house still carries the softness of use. To step inside is to feel the city recede.

Architecture

The calm geometry of the Pombaline.

Lisbon rebuilt itself after 1755 with a new discipline: regular stone façades, tall windows and a timber-framed structure known as the gaiola pombalina.

Palacio Bela Vista belongs to that inheritance. The plan is quadrangular, set on a long rectangular plot. The principal elevation is measured in three storeys; at the garden, the ground falls away and the house gains a fourth.

Stone, wrought iron, azulejo and stucco are used with restraint, each material allowed to show its age. At the centre of the façade, a carved portal and a coat of arms; above, a balustrade against the Lisbon sky. To the side, a wall of blue azulejos turns the corner into the garden.

Interiors

Rooms that keep their own weather.

A sequence of salons, each with a different quality of light: the cool marble of the entrance, the warm hush of the drawing room, the painted sky of the music room.

01 · The arrival

The entrance hall

A floor of black-and-white marble in a lozenge, doors of carved chestnut, a gilt lantern, a pair of red velvet chairs. Beyond the glass, the garden.

The hall is the house in miniature: formal, generous, and made for the slow business of arriving.

02 · The afternoon

The drawing room

Stucco garlands travel across the ceiling; the parquet has gone honey-coloured with time; a Persian rug softens the stone. Tall windows hold the garden at arm's length.

It is a room for tea, for letters, for the hour when Lisbon turns gold.

03 · The evening

The music room

A painted roundel of classical figures presides over a black grand piano. The ceiling is a piece of theatre; the acoustics are warm.

On certain evenings, the house becomes an instrument.

04 · The morning

The garden bedroom

A grey upholstered bed, a blue kilim, a window that opens directly onto the Tagus. Mornings arrive with the river.

The room is quiet in the way that only old houses are quiet: thick walls, deep sashes, the distant sound of the city.

On certain evenings, the palace becomes an instrument.

A private recital in the music room

The hand

The hand, everywhere.

Look closely and the house reveals its makers: the modelled putti, the garlands of fruit, the blue line of a brush on tin-glazed clay, the small stones of the pavement underfoot.

Nothing here is a reproduction. The wear is the signature.

Garden & river

A garden above the river.

Behind the house, a walled garden holds the sun. A stone fountain, a pergola of vines, azulejo benches, a statue on the terrace. Beyond the balustrade, the Tagus, the 25 de Abril bridge and the far shore.

The garden is not a park. It is a room without a ceiling: intimate, enclosed, and entirely private.

Questions

Questions, answered.

Where is Palacio Bela Vista?

At Rua do Ataíde 9, 1200-034 Lisboa, in the parish of Misericórdia, a short walk from the Tagus.

What is a Pombaline palace?

Pombaline describes the architecture of Lisbon after the 1755 earthquake: measured stone façades, tall windows and a timber-framed construction known as the gaiola pombalina. Palacio Bela Vista belongs to that inheritance.

How is the palace arranged?

A quadrangular plan on a long rectangular plot. The principal façade has three storeys; the rear, opening to the garden and the river, has four.

Can the palace be visited?

Yes. Palacio Bela Vista is a private residence, shown by appointment only. Please write to enquiries@palaciobelavista.com to arrange a visit.

Does the palace have a garden?

Yes—a walled garden with a stone fountain, a vine pergola, azulejo benches and a terrace looking over the Tagus.
